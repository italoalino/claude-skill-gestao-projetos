# Choosing and tailoring the approach (predictive, agile, hybrid)

Practical guidance for choosing and combining approaches, written from project management experience. To go deeper on agile practice, see the Agile Practice Guide (PMI and Agile Alliance) and the Scrum Guide.

## Core idea

The goal is to deliver business value in the best way the context allows. Being agile is not the goal. The deciding question is: "how can we be most successful here?". Does frequent feedback help? Delivering in parts helps. Do I need to explore risk while learning? Short cycles help. Can the organization not consume partial value? Agile may not help.

## Life cycles

The table mixes definition with practical heuristics (the "when it fits" and "warning sign" columns). Treat it as a conversation starter, not a rule.

| Cycle | When it fits | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| **Predictive** | Definable work, stable scope, strict regulation or contract, single delivery at the end | Requirements change at every meeting |
| **Iterative** | Unclear requirements; learning happens by refining the product | Nobody knows when to stop |
| **Incremental** | Value can be released in parts; early return to the sponsor | Partial deliveries nobody actually uses |
| **Agile** | High uncertainty, available customer, autonomous team, frequent deliveries | No sponsor who understands the approach; team without authority to decide |
| **Hybrid** | Different parts of the project need different treatment | Becoming "the worst of both worlds" for lack of intent |

## How to combine approaches

Real projects are rarely pure. Three combinations show up often:

1. **By workstream.** The uncertain part (discovery, a new product) runs in short cycles; the regulated, contracted or third-party-dependent part follows a plan with milestones. Define where the workstreams meet (integration milestones).
2. **By phase.** Development under uncertainty in short cycles, followed by a defined, repeatable rollout planned predictively (for example, a new product and then deployment with training for many users).
3. **By transition.** The team first adopts the practices that help it learn and align (short cycles, reviews, retrospectives) and keeps estimation and tracking in the old style. It then moves toward incremental delivery. Culture change takes time, and the larger the organization, the longer it takes.

Combine on purpose, starting from the project's risks. Mixing without criteria produces bureaucracy on both sides.

## Quick questions to set the approach for a new project

Use them in discovery, before writing the plan:

1. How much of the scope is known today and how much will be discovered?
2. Can the customer or user give feedback every few weeks?
3. Can value be delivered in parts, or does it only make sense complete?
4. Are there hard constraints (regulation, fixed-price contract, integration with a partner on its own timeline)?
5. Does the team have the autonomy and experience to decide how to do the work?
6. Is there a sponsor who supports the chosen way of working?
7. What does a mistake cost? A critical or regulated product calls for more verification before release.

Record the answers and the decision in the charter or the approach plan. If the answers split, the approach is hybrid: say which workstreams follow which logic. Any mismatches that appear (for example, a team without autonomy on a project that needs autonomy) go into the risk register and open a conversation with the affected parties.

## Agile practice on real projects

- **PM role in an adaptive environment:** the form changes, not the accountability. Serve the team, remove impediments, take care of stakeholders, risk, communication and alignment with value. Do not decide "how" the team does the work.
- **Backlog:** an ordered list of the work. It does not need to be complete for the whole project: enough for the first release in broad strokes and detail for the next iteration.
- **Refinement:** the product owner and the team prepare upcoming stories before they enter the cycle.
- **Definition of done and of ready:** objective criteria for a story to count as finished or ready to start. They solve much of "the work is never done".
- **Retrospective:** a few improvement items at a time, with a way to measure the result.
- **Demo and review:** the customer sees the product and accepts it bit by bit.
- **Measurement:** velocity (per team, never to compare teams), burn-down and burn-up, flow, cycle time.

## Signs the approach is not working

| Sign | Possible cause | Typical response |
|---|---|---|
| Partial deliveries nobody uses | Value was not agreed with whoever consumes it | Align with the customer on what a useful delivery is before planning the cycle |
| Constant rework | Too much up-front work, or a misunderstood requirement | Deliver less and validate sooner; limit work in progress |
| Scope changing with no record | Changes arrive through side conversations | A single entry channel (backlog or change control, depending on the approach) |
| Team asking approval for everything | Autonomy promised and not given | Define what the team decides alone and what it escalates |
| Meetings with no decisions | A ceremony copied without purpose | Review each meeting's goal; cut what neither decides nor aligns |
| Status always green and a surprise at the end | Weak measurement, or transparency met with fear | A simple, visible metric; treat the messenger with respect |
| Everything "almost done" | No done criteria | Define acceptance criteria per deliverable |

## Tailoring management to each approach

| Topic | Predictive | Agile | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scope | Scope statement, WBS, baseline | Vision, prioritized backlog | Baseline on fixed workstreams, backlog on uncertain ones |
| Change | Integrated control with a change board and impact analysis | Expected; reprioritizes the backlog | Two channels, with an explicit rule for which applies where |
| Schedule | Critical path, milestones | Roadmap, release and iteration | Milestones for external items, cadence for the rest |
| Cost | Baseline and EVM | Incremental funding, spend per iteration | Budget per workstream |
| Acceptance | Formal, per phase | At each review, against the definition of done | Formal where the contract requires it |
| Status | Variance against baseline | Velocity, burn-up, backlog | One report, with the right metric per workstream |
| Risk | Formal register and reserves | Built into the cadence | Single register, reviewed at each workstream's cadence |
