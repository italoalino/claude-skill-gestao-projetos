# Project review checklist

Use in Mode 3. For each domain, look for **evidence**. The rating reflects what can be seen, not what is expected.

## Contents

- Scale
- Questions by domain
- Cross signals (worth more than a single rating)
- Delivery format

## Scale

| Rating | Criterion |
|---|---|
| Green | There is evidence the domain is under control and what exists is current |
| Amber | It exists, but is outdated, incomplete or has a recoverable deviation |
| Red | Significant deviation with no recovery plan, or something essential is missing |
| No evidence | Could not be seen; treat it as a finding and as a question for the PM |

## Questions by domain

### Governance
- Is there an approved charter, with an identified sponsor and a named PM?
- Is it clear who decides what, and the escalation limits and paths?
- Does the integrated plan exist, and are the baselines (when applicable) consistent with each other?
- Do success metrics include value and benefits, not only time and cost?
- Is a change process in use (change board in predictive, reprioritization in agile)? Did recent changes go through it?
- Are lessons learned being recorded during the project?
- Were compliance and sustainability considered (legal, regulatory, contractual requirements)?

### Scope
- Is what is in and out of scope written down and consistent with what the team understands?
- Do requirements have traceability or, in agile, an ordered backlog refined for the next iterations?
- Are there signs of scope creep (requests coming in without going through the process) or gold plating?
- Is there a definition of done or clear acceptance criteria?
- Do deliverables get formal customer validation when needed?

### Schedule
- Are milestones and commitment dates clear and accepted by those involved?
- Is the critical path identified (predictive), or is there a delivery forecast based on velocity and backlog (agile)?
- Does the current deviation have a known cause and trend? Is there a recovery plan with cost and risk stated?
- Are external dependencies (partners, other areas, vendors) mapped and owned?

### Finance
- Is there a budget, a cost baseline and reserves with defined owners?
- Are actual cost and earned value tracked (CPI, SPI, estimate at completion) or, in agile, spend per iteration?
- Were variances above the threshold reported to governance?
- Is there an updated cost forecast at completion?

### Stakeholders
- Has the register been updated in recent cycles? Have new affected parties appeared?
- Is current vs desired engagement assessed for the key stakeholders?
- Do the communication cadence and channels work? Is anyone complaining they did not know about something?
- Are key stakeholders' expectations aligned (definition of success)?

### Resources
- Are roles and responsibilities clear (RACI or equivalent)?
- Actual vs planned availability: is there overload or dependence on a single person?
- Are skill gaps identified and addressed?
- Is the team engaged? Are there impediments the PM has not yet removed?

### Risk
- Does a risk register exist, current, with owners and responses?
- Have materialized risks been moved to issues?
- Are opportunities recorded, not only threats?
- Are reserves consistent with the exposure?
- Are the main risks discussed in the project cadence?

### Quality (within Governance and Scope)
- Are quality criteria defined and tracked?
- Have recurring defects been attacked at the root cause?
- Are tests and reviews in the plan and in the time available?

## Cross signals (worth more than a single rating)

| Pattern | Possible reading |
|---|---|
| Schedule and cost green, scope and risks "no evidence" | The status may look good for lack of measurement. Beware of optimism. |
| Many approved changes with no recorded impact | Change control is not working; cost and schedule will feel it later. |
| Growing delay with an overloaded team | Crashing may make it worse; consider smoothing, cutting scope or renegotiating the date. |
| Issues open for a long time | Authority may have been exceeded; time to escalate with facts. |
| Key stakeholders moving from "neutral" to "resistant" | Adoption risk; the engagement and communication plans need adjusting. |
| Risks never updated, but issues appearing | The risk register became a kickoff document, not a management tool. |
| Promised benefits without measurement | Delivering scope does not guarantee realized value. |

## Delivery format

1. **Scorecard** (table): domain, rating, one-line evidence.
2. **Main findings** (3 to 5): what, why it matters, evidence.
3. **Next 7 days**: actions with a suggested owner and expected result.
4. **Questions for the PM**: what only they know and would change the reading.
5. **Assumptions**: what was assumed in the analysis.

Do not soften a finding to please. If the project is red on something, say so, with the evidence and a way forward.
