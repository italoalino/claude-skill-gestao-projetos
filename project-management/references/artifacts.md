# Artifact skeletons

Use in Mode 2. Each skeleton is a useful minimum, not a form to fill in completely. General rules:

- Fill in only what the user provided or what can be safely inferred. The rest stays **TBD**.
- Fit the size of the project: one page for a small project; open sections for a large one.
- If the PMO has an official template for the artifact, use it and treat these skeletons as a content checklist.
- A document to be read and kept: use the environment's document format. Registers updated every week (risks, issues, stakeholders, changes): spreadsheet or table. Status report: PowerPoint with the neutral template or the organization's (see `status-report.md`).

## Contents

- 1. Project charter
- 2. Discovery (one page)
- 3. Stakeholder register and engagement plan
- 4. Communication plan
- 5. Risk register
- 6. Issue log (issues and impediments)
- 7. Change request
- 8. Meeting minutes
- 9. Lessons learned (during and at the end)
- 10. Approach plan (short)
- 11. Closure report

## 1. Project charter

Authorizes the project and ties it to the business objective. The sponsor approves it; the PM is named here. It does not detail scope or schedule.

1. Project name, originating request (number, if any), date
2. Sponsor and PM
3. Justification and business objective (which problem or opportunity)
4. Expected outcomes and **how the benefit will be measured** (indicator, baseline, target)
5. High-level scope: what is in and what is **not**
6. Main deliverables and summary milestones
7. Assumptions and constraints
8. High-level initial risks
9. Key stakeholders
10. High-level budget and timeline estimates (a range, not a fixed number, during discovery)
11. Proposed approach (predictive, agile, hybrid) and why
12. Success criteria and acceptance criteria
13. PM authority and escalation limits
14. Approval (name, role, date)

## 2. Discovery (one page)

Between the PM assignment and the plan. Answers "is it worth it and how do we run it?".

- The problem and who suffers from it
- Desired outcome and metric
- What is known and what is not
- Stakeholders heard and still to hear
- Systems, areas and partners involved (dependencies)
- Constraints (regulatory, contractual, date)
- First answers to the approach questions (`approaches.md`)
- Open risks and issues
- Recommendation: proceed, adjust scope or stop; next steps and owners

## 3. Stakeholder register and engagement plan

Spreadsheet, one row per stakeholder. The register is visible, so **no sensitive data or personal opinions**.

| Column | Note |
|---|---|
| Name or group, area, role in the project | Prefer the group when there are many people |
| Interest (H/M/L) and influence (H/M/L) | Basis for the power x interest grid |
| Main expectation | What this person expects from the project |
| Current and desired engagement | Unaware, resistant, neutral, supportive, leading |
| Engagement strategy | What to do to move from current to desired |
| Channel, frequency, communication owner | Feeds the communication plan |

## 4. Communication plan

| Information | Audience | Purpose | Channel | Frequency | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Add: how feedback is received (communication is two-way), the escalation pattern, and where the official information lives. If the PMO has a defined status cadence, use it as the starting point.

## 5. Risk register

| Column | Note |
|---|---|
| ID, date, description | Write as "cause, event, effect" |
| Type | Threat or opportunity |
| Probability and impact (H/M/L or the plan's scale) | Qualitative prioritization; quantify only the critical ones |
| Trigger | Sign the risk is approaching |
| Response and strategy | Threat: escalate, avoid, transfer, mitigate, accept. Opportunity: escalate, exploit, share, enhance, accept |
| Owner, due date, status | One owner per risk |
| Associated reserve | Contingency, when there is one |

Reminder: a risk that has materialized leaves this register and goes to the issue log.

## 7. Change request

One page, used in predictive work (and in hybrid work for baselined workstreams).

- ID, date, requester, project
- Description of the change and reason (problem or opportunity)
- Alternatives considered
- **Impact**: scope, schedule, cost, quality, risk, resources, benefits
- PM recommendation
- Decision (approve, reject, defer), decision maker, date
- Documents to update and who communicates

In agile, the same information becomes a backlog item with value, estimated effort and priority agreed with the product owner.

## 8. Meeting minutes

- Date, attendees, purpose
- Decisions (who decided, what)
- Actions (what, owner, due date)
- New risks or issues
- Open points for next time

End with the actions and due dates highlighted. Good minutes fit on half a page.

## 9. Lessons learned (during and at the end)

| Situation | What worked | What did not | Root cause | Recommendation | Who can benefit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Record during the project, not only at closing. At closing, run the retrospective before releasing the team.

## 10. Approach plan (short)

When the project is hybrid or there is doubt: which workstream follows which approach, how change enters each one, which status metric each uses, and where the workstreams meet (integration milestones).

## 11. Closure report

- Deliverables and formal acceptance (who accepted, when)
- Results against success criteria and measured benefit (or plan to measure later)
- Open items and post-project owners
- Transition readiness (operations, support)
- Administrative closure (contracts, finance, released resources, archive)
- Lessons learned
