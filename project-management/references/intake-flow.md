# Project intake flow

This file describes a typical way a project reaches the PM. Every organization has its own: treat this as a starting point, not a rule. If the user describes their organization's flow, follow what they said.

**Customize:** edit this file with your organization's real flow (stage names, who approves, status cadence, official templates). The more concrete it is, the better the recommendations.

## Typical flow

1. Demand arrives as a **project request** (in many companies, a standard form or document written by the requesting area).
2. The request goes through **triage or prioritization** (PMO, portfolio committee or area manager), which approves it and **assigns the PM**.
3. The PM starts **discovery**: understanding the real problem, scope, stakeholders, dependencies and constraints.
4. Discovery produces the **project charter** and the approach decision (predictive, agile or hybrid).

Practical consequence: the PM almost always starts from a request written by someone else. A useful first step is to compare what the request asks for with what discovery finds and record the differences. Use the discovery skeleton in `artifacts.md`.

## Status cadence

Cadence varies by project and organization; weekly is the most common. If the user does not say what the cadence is for a given project, ask before building the status.

## Templates

- If the organization has official artifact templates, they take precedence. Ask before generating.
- Without official templates, use the skeletons in `artifacts.md` and, for the PowerPoint status, the neutral template in `assets/template.pptx` (or the organization's, prepared as described in `status-report.md`).

## What to ask when information is missing

If a fact is neither here nor in the conversation, **do not assume**. Ask the user, or suggest they confirm with the PMO or governance, and record the answer in the project artifact. The most common gaps:

- Who approves the project and who the sponsor is
- The PM's authority limits (financial, schedule, scope)
- Change approval flow (is there a change board? who decides?)
- Committee calendar and leadership reporting schedule
- The official project tool (where the schedule, backlog and risks live)
